# Na szychcie od 1915 — research and design decision

Research date: 11 November 2026. Full form only. This is an engineering concept, not a
released or physically tested product. The target is approximately 180 × 90 × 120 mm.

## What makes it Carnall

The museum confirms the builder Eisenhütte Prinz Rudolph, 1915 and 2,000 KM. The city's
technical description identifies **two horizontal cylinders and a 6 m Koepe friction wheel**.
That distinction changes the design: a winding drum would eventually fill or empty; a
continuous cord passing over a friction wheel can keep running in either direction.
The model should celebrate this visible cause and effect. “Winds” in the original brief is
interpreted as rope travel, not layers accumulating on a drum. [1, 2]

The heritage register describes brick machine halls, shallow pitched roofs and pilasters.
It records a riveted headframe with a diagonal support and two parallel sheaves. This concept
compresses that vocabulary into two brick walls, tall openings, a short roof fragment and
**one working sheave**. It is a stylised interpretation, not a scale replica. Arched openings
are a deliberate regional industrial shorthand; the museum's site photograph shows many
rectangular multi-pane windows. Do not present the arches as surveyed Carnall architecture. [3]

The museum's engine photo shows dark painted castings, polished moving rods, red details
and brass-coloured oilers. The coloured theatrical lighting prevents reliable paint matching.
Use iron charcoal, brass ochre, warm brick and cream for the concept. Confirm real paint,
wheel/spoke shape, cylinder arrangement and rope exit during the visit. [4, 5]

## What could make it sell

There is no sales evidence here proving a “winning formula”. These are design hypotheses
informed by existing products and the museum subject:

| Observation | Decision for this souvenir | How to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Ugears offers a small working pneumatic engine with visible piston action. [6] | Make the engine itself the attraction; ship assembled so the first turn is immediate. | Counter demo: can a visitor understand it without instruction? |
| Kikkerland exposes a small crank mechanism; the NYPL museum-style shop sells it as a gift. [7, 8] | One obvious ochre crank grip, no power switch or battery. | Test grip comfort, effort and both directions. |
| Cabaret Mechanical Theatre exhibits and sells contemporary automata. [9] | Use a calm plinth and a deliberate cutaway, so it reads as a small mechanical exhibit. | Compare shelf appeal with static regional souvenirs. |
| Carnall visitors can watch the real machine in action. [1] | Recreate the remembered sequence: crank → wheel → rods → sliders → rope → sheave. | Ask visitors which feature they recognise after the demonstration. |
| The object is a gift and a travel purchase. | Keep all motion inside the footprint, use a removable headframe and rope for servicing, and provide a fitted carton. | Bag/drop trials and an assembly-time trial. |

Prioritise a large readable wheel, two contrasting moving rods, open cylinder windows that
reveal the piston heads, a quiet reversible action, and a legible **CARNALL / ZABRZE** plate.
Keep the roof and front wall cut away. The brass accents must indicate motion or identity,
not turn into decorative steampunk clutter. Do not add coal wagons, smoke, a motor or a cage
to this edition. The sheave is the backdrop; *The Shift Change* can retain the cage narrative.

## Motion selected

Direct hand crank on the main shaft; twin slider-cranks offset by 90° as a **design choice**,
not a verified Carnall crank angle. Two open-top cylinder shells reveal translating piston
heads. Both crossheads run on paired rails. Connecting rods swing on clearance pivots.
The main wheel and upper sheave share one closed, externally tangent cord loop; the smaller
sheave turns faster. An ochre witness bead in the visualisation makes rope travel readable.
The cord is bought-in flexible material, not a rigid printed belt.

Use a shallow cord groove and an adjustable upper axle seat for tension. A slipping cord
is preferable to a locked mechanism; tune grip in a physical prototype. The CAD and animation
prove geometric relationships only, not friction, tactile quality or life. All declared moving
parts need mechanical retention; a removable crank and axle caps provide access.

## Copy and range

Default display plate: **CARNALL · ZABRZE / NA SZYCHCIE OD 1915**.
Heritage card: **“2,000 KM. On shift since 1915.”** KM is the source unit (metric horsepower);
“2,000 hp” is acceptable loose marketing translation, not an exact unit conversion.
Optional reverse of card: **“On shift since 1915. Still no pension.”** Keep the joke off the
permanent model. “Since 1915” is a heritage strapline, not a claim of uninterrupted operation;
the city's account says the engine served the shaft until 1990. [2]

Provide a separately printed, two-pin nameplate. Rybnik's Ignacy is a credible future branch:
its operator documents a working winding engine from **1920**. Do not carry the Carnall date
or power onto other machines. Katowice and Tarnowskie Góry remain unverified retail/range
ideas, not claims that matching engines operate there. Each site needs its own facts and
approval for any museum branding. [10]

## Prototype acceptance before sale

1. Print the shaft/bearing and slider/rail coupons in the actual material and orientation.
2. Assemble without forcing; verify pin retention, rope tension and access to the crank.
3. Run 500 turns in each direction; inspect rub marks, seizure and cord derailment.
4. Try slow starts at eight crank positions and check that the plinth stays stable.
5. Visitor trial: recognition, comprehension, preferred copy and willingness to buy.
6. Confirm packaging protection, actual production time and assembly cost before setting price.

The initial fit allowance is a prototype parameter, not a guarantee for every printer. Small
parts and exposed mechanisms mean this should be developed as a display collectible; any
age grading and market requirements need a separate product assessment before sale.
