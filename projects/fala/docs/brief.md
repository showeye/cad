# Brief: "Fala" (The Wave), a kinetic Silesian souvenir (full piece)

*Client brief, saved verbatim on 2026-11-10. This is the source of truth for the project.*

## Your role
You are the product designer and mechanical engineer for a small Silesian souvenir brand. Take this concept from idea to a print-ready, assemblable, working object, plus marketing renders. You decide how it looks in detail and how it works. This brief only says what it must achieve, and why.

## The story
Upper Silesia sits on top of coal mines. When the coal is extracted, the ground above sinks and shifts (mining damage, "szkody górnicze"). Streets ripple, houses lean and crack, and residents spend years arguing over compensation.

Fala turns this into a small fairground ride: a street of familoks (the traditional red-brick workers' houses of the region) standing on ground that rolls like a wave when you turn a crank.

- Card line: "In Silesia the ground moves. The compensation doesn't."
- Tone: dry, affectionate local humour. It should make people smile. It must not look like a disaster scene: no figures in danger, no destruction.
- Buyers: mostly foreign visitors to Katowice and the region, plus local collectors. It is sold as a numbered small-run collector piece, so it must feel like a premium object, not a toy.

## What the person experiences
1. At rest, it reads instantly as a charming street of old Silesian brick houses on ground that is gently out of true. It must look good as a still photo at any point in the motion.
2. They turn a crank or handle at one end. A wave travels along the street: houses rise, sink and lean in sequence. The ground and pavement in front of them move with them, so the earth itself seems to roll.
3. The motion is slow, smooth and continuous, a ripple rather than a jolt. Turning continuously loops seamlessly, with no visible start or end to the cycle. Turning backwards also works and sends the wave the other way.
4. Effort is light and even through the whole turn. An elderly tourist should be able to turn it with two fingers. Nothing catches, clicks, rattles or squeaks.
5. Whatever makes it move is invisible from normal viewing angles: front, three-quarter, and from above at shop-counter height. The underside and the back may show it.
6. The movement is obvious from 1 to 2 m away in a shop window, but it stays believable. The houses stay recognisably one terrace. Neighbouring houses may shift against each other, but the street never shows distracting gaps, light leaks or exposed internals between them.

## The look: research it first
No reference images come with this brief. Working out the look is part of your job, and it matters most: the piece sells on how it looks in a shop window and in a photo. Before proposing any mechanism, research the following, using real photos and sources rather than other people's renders.

1. **Real familoks.** Study Silesian workers' estates such as Nikiszowiec and Giszowiec in Katowice and Ficinus in Ruda Śląska, plus any others you find. Identify what makes a familok instantly recognisable: brick colour and coursing, window shapes and surrounds, frame colours, cornices and bands, roofs, chimneys, doors and entrances, pavements, and the proportions of a terrace. Separate the features that are essential from those that can be dropped at this scale.
2. **Real mining damage.** Find out how subsided streets and buildings actually look: tilting, cracking, bracing, steel ties, uneven pavements, new ponds. Decide which signs to exaggerate so a visitor reads "the ground moves" at a glance, while the piece stays light-hearted.
3. **Wave motion that reads.** Look at kinetic sculpture and automata that show waves, and work out the visual principles: how many elements, how much rise and lean, how far the wave travels per turn, the speed, and what makes motion feel organic rather than mechanical. Take principles, not designs: do not copy any artist's work.
4. **What looks good printed.** Look at photos of real multi-colour FDM prints of buildings and stylised collectibles, not renders. Work out which styles, detail sizes and colour splits come out well and which look cheap, and use that to set the level of stylisation.
5. **The market.** Find which souvenirs of Nikiszowiec and Silesia already exist, so Fala looks clearly different and more premium.

Turn the research into a **look brief**:
- 15–25 linked references, each with one line on what to take from it;
- the recommended stylisation, proportions and exaggeration;
- the palette, with reasons, within the colour limit below;
- the motion described in visual terms (how much movement, where, how fast it feels);
- 2–3 quick concept visuals of the look for me to choose from.

Cite your sources and flag anything you could not verify.

## Fixed starting points (change these only with a stated reason)
- Style: flat, matte, chunky stylisation. Shape and relief carry the detail, never paint. No weathering, grime, soot or gradients: the printer can't make them and we do not paint.
- Subject: a terrace of several familoks with the pavement in front, on a base that hints at the mine underneath. Your research decides the number of houses, the street details and the design of the base.
- Size: about 200 mm long, 70 mm deep and 110 mm tall including the motion, ±15% if it improves the result. It must sit steady on a shelf, without wobbling or walking, while being cranked.

## Production constraints (hard limits)
- Printer: Snapmaker U1, an FDM toolchanger with four toolheads, so at most four colours in one print job. 0.4 mm nozzle, build volume 270 × 270 × 270 mm.
- Material: PLA only, for every printed part, moving parts included. Colours can be mixed freely; other filament types cannot.
- Every printed part uses at most 4 colours, and colour boundaries fall where they look intentional.
- No painting, sanding or finishing. What comes off the plate is what the customer sees, apart from removing supports from hidden faces.
- Visible faces have no support scars and no seams where they show. Hide layer lines with texture or orientation wherever possible.
- Every visible feature must print cleanly and survive handling at this scale with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Nothing so fine that it blobs or snaps.
- Bought hardware is allowed if it is cheap, standard and easy to source in the EU (for example pins, screws, springs, magnets, bearings). Keep the count low and justify each item.
- One person assembles a unit in 15 minutes or less with ordinary hand tools. No glue in anything that moves. A worn or broken part can be replaced without destroying the piece.
- Production runs are 20–50 units. Keep the total print time per unit reasonable and report it. Share identical parts wherever possible so plates are efficient.

## Durability and handling
- It will sit on shop counters as a demo and be cranked by strangers. Target at least 10,000 full turns without noticeable wear, loosening, squeaking or loss of smoothness.
- Cranking fast, over-cranking or forcing it backwards must not break it.
- Nothing fragile sticks out. It must survive packing and postal shipping in a compact box, with nothing loose inside.
- It must tolerate a warm shop window in summer without sagging, warping or seizing, even though PLA softens at around 55–60 °C.

## How to work
1. **Research the look** as described above, and write the look brief.
2. **Concept checkpoint. Stop here for my approval.** Present the look brief and its 2–3 concept visuals. Then propose 2–3 genuinely different ways to produce the motion. For each, give a short description, a simple diagram, how it meets the experience goals, the main risks, the part count, the bought hardware and an estimated print time. Recommend one look and one approach, and explain why. Ask me about anything this brief leaves open. Do not model in detail before I approve.
3. **Motion proof.** Before decorating anything, build the moving system on its own. Simulate it through full rotations in both directions, and show there are no collisions, that effort stays even and that the wave reads as the look brief intends. Give me a short animation of the bare mechanism.
4. **Test prints.** Give me a small set of test parts that check the riskiest fits and motions on my U1, plus one sample house section to confirm the look. Tell me what to check and what to send back (photos, measurements) so you can tune the design.
5. **Full design.** Model the decorated piece to the approved look brief. Keep every critical dimension and fit as a named, adjustable parameter, so it can be tuned after test prints without remodelling.
6. **Production package and marketing assets**, as listed below.

## Deliverables
- The look brief, with its references and concept visuals.
- The parametric source model and STEP files of every part.
- Print-ready 3MF plates for Snapmaker Orca. Each plate includes part orientation, PLA colour per toolhead, and supports (if any) only on hidden faces, with recommended settings, estimated print time and filament use.
- A bill of materials: printed parts with quantities and PLA colours, and bought hardware with standard sizes.
- A step-by-step assembly guide with exploded views, ending with a check that proves the unit runs correctly.
- A Blender scene whose materials match what the U1 really prints: flat matte PLA, fine layer texture, no painted effects. Include a seamless looping animation of the wave being cranked, a 360° turntable, and three stills (hero three-quarter, front, close-up detail).
- A risk list: what is most likely to go wrong in production, and how the design guards against it.

## Definition of done
- Someone who has never been to Silesia sees old Silesian workers' houses on moving ground, and gets the joke with the one-line card.
- The piece follows the approved look brief.
- Turning the crank sends one smooth, continuous wave along every house, in both directions, with no collisions anywhere in the rotation.
- Every printed part is PLA, fits the U1, uses 4 colours or fewer, needs no paint and shows no support scars.
- The mechanism is hidden from front, three-quarter and top views at counter height.
- No distracting gaps open between houses at any point in the cycle.
- A unit assembles in 15 minutes or less, using only standard hardware, and its moving parts can be serviced.
- It stays put on a flat surface while being cranked, and nothing fragile protrudes.
- The renders match the look of the real print, not an idealised painted model.

## Out of scope for now
The fridge-magnet version, the packaging and the story card design.
