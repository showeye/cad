# Brief: "Serce na węgiel" (Coal-Powered Heart), a kinetic Silesian souvenir (full piece)

## Your role
You are the product designer and mechanical engineer for a small Silesian souvenir brand. Take this concept from idea to a print-ready, assemblable, working object, plus marketing renders. You decide how it looks in detail, how it opens and how it works. This brief only says what it must achieve, and why.

## The story
For more than a century, coal was the heart of Upper Silesia. It paid the wages, heated the family kitchen and shaped the region's identity. Today the region is debating how, and how fast, to leave coal behind.

Serce na węgiel is that heart made literal: a lump of coal in the shape of a heart that opens to reveal a warm, lived-in Silesian home inside. Hard and black outside, warm inside.

- Card line: "Still runs on coal."
- Tone: wry and affectionate. It is a love letter to the region with a small jab at the energy debate. It takes no political side, and it must never feel like a lament or a protest piece.
- Buyers: mostly foreign visitors to Katowice and the region, plus local collectors. It is sold as a numbered small-run collector piece and is the flagship of the range, so it must feel like a precious, precise object, closer to a jewellery box than a toy.

## What the person experiences
1. **Closed**, it reads at a glance as a heart carved from coal. It is a striking dark sculpture on a desk or shelf in its own right. Nothing hints that it opens: whatever lines it has look like natural features of the surface, not doors or joints.
2. **One simple hand action** opens it, ideally something done with the base, such as turning it. Nobody should need instructions beyond a single word on the card.
3. **The opening is the show.** The shell opens gradually, evenly and under the person's control, as a small theatrical reveal: there is a moment of surprise when the light first gets in, then the whole room comes into view. It must look beautiful at every point of the opening, not just at the start and end.
4. **Open**, the interior is fully visible from the front and front three-quarter view, and the opened shell frames it attractively. The contrast between the black coal and the warm room is the point of the piece.
5. **Closing** reverses the motion smoothly and ends in a tight, convincing closed form. The piece stays wherever it is left (open, closed or in between). It never flops, springs or slams.
6. **The feel** is precise and quiet: even resistance, no backlash, no rattling, nothing that can be forced past its limits. Whatever makes it move stays invisible in both the closed and open states.
7. **Handling:** people will pick it up and turn it over. It must not open by accident, shed parts or pinch fingers when they do.

## The look: research it first
No reference images come with this brief. Working out the look is part of your job, and it matters most: the piece sells on the closed sculpture and on the moment it opens. Before proposing any mechanism, research the following, using real photos and sources rather than other people's renders.

1. **Coal itself.** How hard coal and anthracite really look: lustre, fracture, facets, edges. Find out how Silesia's tradition of carving figures from coal treats the material. Decide what form and surface say "coal" most clearly, and how close black PLA can get to it.
2. **The heart form.** Anatomical heart, symbolic heart or a blend: which reads fastest to a foreign visitor while still feeling surprising and premium? How much stylisation keeps it elegant rather than gory or cute?
3. **The Silesian home inside.** Study historic Silesian workers' homes and kitchens, as preserved in museums and heritage estates such as Nikiszowiec. Identify the handful of objects and details that make a room unmistakably Silesian, cosy and lived-in at very small scale. Decide whether the room shows people, or only signs of them.
4. **Reveals that delight.** Study objects whose opening is the experience: surprise eggs, opening flower lamps, iris and puzzle boxes, kinetic opening sculptures. Work out what makes a reveal feel magical (pacing, order, what is seen first, how the opening frames the inside). Take principles, not designs: do not copy any artist's or brand's work.
5. **What looks good printed.** Look at photos of real black and multi-colour PLA prints, not renders. Find out how faceted dark surfaces, layer lines, seams and tiny interior details actually come out, and use that to set the stylisation and scale of the details.
6. **The market.** Find which heart-themed and coal-themed souvenirs of Silesia already exist, so this piece is clearly different and more premium.

Turn the research into a **look brief**:
- 15–25 linked references, each with one line on what to take from it;
- the recommended form of the heart, its surface treatment and its stylisation;
- the interior scene and its key objects;
- the palette, with reasons, within the colour limit below;
- the opening described in visual terms (how it unfolds, what is seen when, how the open shell frames the room);
- 2–3 quick concept visuals of the closed and open heart for me to choose from.

Cite your sources and flag anything you could not verify.

## Fixed starting points (change these only with a stated reason)
- Exterior: black coal. Faceted, low-poly surfaces are a good starting point because they suit the material and help hide layer lines and joints. Your research decides the final form and facet treatment.
- Interior: a warm, lived-in Silesian room in flat warm colours. Its warmth comes from colour and composition. Keep the base design free of electronics; if you believe a light would transform the piece, propose it as an optional variant.
- Style: flat colours, shape and relief carry the detail, never paint. No weathering, gradients or painted effects.
- Size: about 100 mm tall including the base, ±15% if it improves the result. It must sit steady while being operated.

## Production constraints (hard limits)
- Printer: Snapmaker U1, an FDM toolchanger with four toolheads, so at most four colours in one print job. 0.4 mm nozzle, build volume 270 × 270 × 270 mm.
- Material: PLA only, for every printed part, moving parts included. Colours can be mixed freely; other filament types cannot.
- Every printed part uses at most 4 colours, and colour boundaries fall where they look intentional.
- No painting, sanding or finishing. What comes off the plate is what the customer sees, apart from removing supports from hidden faces.
- Visible faces have no support scars. Joints and seams on the closed heart fall where they read as part of the surface.
- Every visible feature, inside and out, must print cleanly and survive handling at this scale with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Nothing so fine that it blobs or snaps.
- Bought hardware is allowed if it is cheap, standard and easy to source in the EU (for example pins, screws, springs, magnets, bearings). Keep the count low and justify each item.
- One person assembles a unit in 20 minutes or less with ordinary hand tools. No glue in anything that moves. A worn or broken part can be replaced without destroying the piece.
- Production runs are 20–50 units. Keep the total print time per unit reasonable and report it. Share identical parts wherever possible so plates are efficient.

## Durability and handling
- It will sit on shop counters as a demo and be opened by strangers. Target at least 5,000 full open-and-close cycles without noticeable wear, loosening, squeaking or loss of precision.
- Opening or closing too fast, or forcing it past either end, must not break it.
- It must survive packing and postal shipping in a compact box, with nothing loose inside.
- It must tolerate a warm shop window in summer without sagging, warping or seizing, even though PLA softens at around 55–60 °C.

## How to work
1. **Research the look** as described above, and write the look brief.
2. **Concept checkpoint. Stop here for my approval.** Present the look brief and its 2–3 concept visuals. Then propose 2–3 genuinely different ways for the heart to open and to be driven. For each, give a short description, a simple diagram or animation sketch, how it meets the experience goals, the main risks, the part count, the bought hardware and an estimated print time. Recommend one look and one approach, and explain why. Ask me about anything this brief leaves open. Do not model in detail before I approve.
3. **Motion proof.** Before decorating anything, build the moving system and the shell as plain shapes. Simulate the full opening and closing, and show that no parts collide, that the shell closes tight, that the effort stays even and that the reveal reads as the look brief intends. Give me a short animation of this bare version.
4. **Test prints.** Give me a small set of test parts that check the riskiest fits and motions on my U1, plus one sample of the coal surface and one interior detail to confirm the look. Tell me what to check and what to send back (photos, measurements) so you can tune the design.
5. **Full design.** Model the decorated piece to the approved look brief. Keep every critical dimension and fit as a named, adjustable parameter, so it can be tuned after test prints without remodelling.
6. **Production package and marketing assets**, as listed below.

## Deliverables
- The look brief, with its references and concept visuals.
- The parametric source model and STEP files of every part.
- Print-ready 3MF plates for Snapmaker Orca. Each plate includes part orientation, PLA colour per toolhead, and supports (if any) only on hidden faces, with recommended settings, estimated print time and filament use.
- A bill of materials: printed parts with quantities and PLA colours, and bought hardware with standard sizes.
- A step-by-step assembly guide with exploded views, ending with a check that proves the unit opens and closes correctly.
- A Blender scene whose materials match what the U1 really prints: flat matte or satin PLA, fine layer texture, no painted effects. Include a seamless looping open-and-close animation, a 360° turntable of the closed heart, and four stills (closed hero, half open, fully open, close-up of the interior).
- A risk list: what is most likely to go wrong in production, and how the design guards against it.

## Definition of done
- Closed, someone who has never been to Silesia sees a heart made of coal. Open, they see a warm Silesian home, and they get the joke with the one-line card.
- The piece follows the approved look brief.
- One simple hand action opens and closes it smoothly, with no collisions at any point, and it stays where it is left.
- Closed, the shell is tight and its joints read as part of the coal surface.
- The mechanism is invisible in both the open and closed states.
- Every printed part is PLA, fits the U1, uses 4 colours or fewer, needs no paint and shows no support scars.
- A unit assembles in 20 minutes or less, using only standard hardware, and its moving parts can be serviced.
- It cannot open by accident when picked up, sheds no parts and pinches no fingers.
- The renders match the look of the real print, not an idealised painted model.

## Out of scope for now
The fridge-magnet version, the packaging and the story card design.
